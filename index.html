II. The legend

The night the music stopped.

In 1776 a Portuguese merchant family raised a house among the palms of Versova. It had whitewashed walls, arched windows, a deep stone well in the yard, and a hall wide enough for a hundred dancers.

Dona Isabel de Mello, the lady of the house, set the date for the house-warming: the thirtieth of October. She invited everyone who mattered and a few who didn’t. The bandmaster tuned his strings. The boatman rowed guests in across the creek. Her twin daughters wore matching white.

At midnight the music stopped mid-note, and nobody left. No one in Versova agrees on why. Some blame the well, some blame the wine, and some say Dona Isabel simply refused to let a good party end.

For 250 years the house has kept its guests. Once a year, on the thirtieth of October, the doors open, the band picks up the note it dropped, and the living are let in until midnight.

Welcome to Ghost Town.
